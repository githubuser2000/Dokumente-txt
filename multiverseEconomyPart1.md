# The Four Elements: Rock Paper Scissors, Tic Tac Toe, or Connect Four?

The interaction between Water, Air, Earth, and Fire can be viewed through the lens of all three games, as each one represents a different aspect of their relationship.

---

### 1. Like "Rock, Paper, Scissors" (The Dynamics)
This is the most accurate comparison for the **balance of power**. In many systems (like RPGs or mythology), one element counters another but is weak against a third.

* **Fire** is extinguished by **Water**.
* **Water** carves through **Earth** or is contained by it.
* **Earth** is eroded by **Air** (Wind).
* There is no "ultimate" element; each has a specific advantage and a fatal flaw.

### 2. Like "Tic Tac Toe" (The Duality)
Tic Tac Toe is built on **binary opposites** (X vs. O). In classical philosophy (e.g., Aristotle), elements are often arranged in polar pairs:

* **Fire vs. Water** (Hot/Dry vs. Cold/Wet)
* **Air vs. Earth** (Warm/Moist vs. Cold/Dry)
* Much like Tic Tac Toe, when these direct opposites clash, they often result in a **stalemate** or mutual cancellation rather than a productive mixture.

### 3. Like "Connect Four" (The Completeness)
This represents **structure and combination**. 

* In alchemy and ancient science, the world only exists when **all four** elements work together.
* Like "Connect Four," the system is only "won" or complete when all pieces are in place. It’s not about fighting each other, but about how they **stack** and complement one another to create matter and life.

---

### Summary

| Game | Elemental Aspect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Rock, Paper, Scissors** | **Interaction** (Who beats whom?). |
| **Tic Tac Toe** | **Polarity** (The irreconcilable opposites). |
| **Connect Four** | **Wholeness** (All four are required for the system). |
